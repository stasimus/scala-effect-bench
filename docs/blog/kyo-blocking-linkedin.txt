Kyo's strong results against CE3 in several synthetic benchmarks convinced me to try it in a real service. Then I noticed what my benchmark had left out: blocking operations.

In a separate blocking TCP benchmark, Kyo was about 30x slower than cats-effect. Calling flush() before each blocking exchange changed that dramatically.

Kyo went from 5 to 82 batches/s. With worker and placement tuning too, it reached 145, close to CE's default 149, with overlapping confidence intervals.

The interesting catch: the flush was manual. Kyo's Finagle integration already does it in its blocking hook.

Nonblocking results were much closer: Kyo led CE by about 12% at concurrency 8 and trailed by about 3% at 64.

I still like Kyo. How much scheduler knowledge should application code need?
